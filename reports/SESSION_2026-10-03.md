### 1b. The import chain is broken for good

Sending an email does not need the model framework — it needs the coefficients. New
`score_l.py` implements the exported JSON's own six-step scoring recipe on **`json`, `math`
and `bisect` only**. The proof is stronger than "joblib is now installed": the entire live
path imports and every self-test passes in a venv that has **no joblib at all**.

`score_l.self_test()` — 14 checks — confirms the JSON reproduces the joblib bundle's
verdicts exactly, that calibrated probability is monotone in subscription, and that
importing it pulls in no modelling package (checked in a child interpreter, not by
inspecting its own source).

It also measured something worth knowing: the threshold rule's two stated forms — isotonic
`p_cal >= required` and the shortcut `p_raw >= raw_equivalent_threshold` — fire within
**0.37% (L1) and 0.35% (L2)** of the same Total subscription. They are not identical,
because the shortcut sits *on* an isotonic knot while the step function jumps *at* it.
Production uses the isotonic route.

**Where the bar actually is: L1 fires from Total ≥ 64.9x, L2 from ≥ 98.5x.**

## Priority 7 — the L2 threshold

Recorded as **H16, needs ratification**. Rather than put two thin readings side by side, I
widened the out-of-sample window to see whether the shortfall survives a larger fire count.

| Window | Fires | Hits | Delivered | Wilson 95% LB | Meets 80%? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 months | 25 | 19 | **76.0%** | 56.6% | no |
| 18 months | 38 | 32 | 84.2% | 69.6% | yes |
| 24 months | 54 | 48 | 88.9% | 77.8% | yes |
| **36 months** | **125** | **97** | **77.6%** | 69.5% | **no** |
| **48 months** | **138** | **106** | **76.8%** | 69.1% | **no** |
| **60 months** | **160** | **125** | **78.1%** | 71.1% | **no** |

**It is not small-sample noise.** At 125–160 fires the delivered rate sits at 76.8–78.1%.
The 18- and 24-month windows are the exceptions, and the 24-month figure (88.9%) is the
2024–25 hot market — the same window that produced H11's false positive on issue size.

**L1 is the control and clears its promise in every window:** 92.3% / 92.9% / 95.2% / 91.0%
at 39–145 fires. So this is specific to L2's threshold, not general degradation.

**The derivation already said so.** Stage 16 chose L2's threshold on a delivered 85.1% over
94 fires whose **Wilson 95% lower bound was 76.5%**, passing C5 only because the rule allows
5pp of slack. 76–78% is that lower bound materialising. The central estimate was the
optimistic read all along.

**Recommendation: re-derive L2's threshold at Gate B.** Re-deriving by the C5 rule on the
last 36 months gives 0.84 (0.82 at 48 months), delivering **92.2% on 64 fires** — at the
cost of roughly **half the bids**. Those figures come from models refitted on each window's
training half, so the thresholds are not transplantable to the production bundle; the
re-derivation must be done on the production fit.

**Until that is done, L2's stated 80% is not supported by the data. L1's 90% is.** The
email prints both thresholds from the model export rather than from a hardcoded number, so
whatever you decide will flow through without editing the report.
